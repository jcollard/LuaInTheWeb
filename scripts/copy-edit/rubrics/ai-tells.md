# Your focus: does this sound like an AI wrote it?

You're an editor who has read thousands of AI-written texts and can spot them right away. Find every passage that a careful reader would recognize as machine-written, and suggest a rewrite that sounds like a real teacher wrote it.

Look for:
- Stock phrases: "dive in", "let's explore", "it's important to note", "it's worth mentioning", "in this guide, we will", "by the end of this part", "happy coding", "and that's it", "under the hood", "seamlessly", "robust", "leverage", "delve", "embark", "journey", "unlock", "empower".
- Patterns in sentence shape: the "not just X, but Y" pattern, lists of three by habit, rhetorical questions answered right away ("What does this do? It..."), and many sentences in a row with the same length or rhythm.
- Em-dashes used for asides, definitions, or drama. This includes the "term — explanation" pattern in concept lists.
- Sections ending with a recap of what was just done, or a cheerful line that adds nothing.
- Bold, headings, or bullet lists used where plain sentences would read better.
- Praise or excitement that's generic rather than about the specific thing the student's game now does.
- Hedging and filler that pads the text without adding information.
- Vocabulary that's too polished or formal for a teacher talking to a kid.

Don't flag plain, clear sentences just because they're plain. Plain is good. Labels the page uses on purpose ("TYPE THIS CODE", "CHALLENGE", "Where:", "Check Your Work") are part of the layout, not AI tells.
