# Your focus: does the guide talk to one student in one steady voice?

The guide speaks directly to a single student as "you", in plain, friendly sentences. There is no first-person teacher ("I"), and that's on purpose. Find every place where the voice slips.

Look for:
- Addressing a group: "everyone", "students", "learners", "you all", "class".
- Too much "we" or "let's". It's fine now and then for doing something together ("We'll write new versions of both in the next steps"), but not as the default voice.
- A first-person "I" from the writer. The guide doesn't use one.
- Textbook or documentation voice: passive sentences ("the function is called"), impersonal statements ("one should"), formal register.
- Product or marketing voice: hype, selling the feature, superlatives.
- Bare commands with no reason for a long stretch, so the text becomes a list of orders. A short "why" after something works keeps the student with you.
- A voice that changes between sections, such as warm in one part and cold and technical in the next, or hints that sound different from the task text.
- Places where naming a common mistake would help: "If the ship disappears, check that you drew it after the background."

Suggest rewrites in a consistent, warm, plain "you" voice.
