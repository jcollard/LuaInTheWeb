# Your focus: does this make the student feel capable?

The students are 10 to 14 and might be struggling alone. The guide should leave them feeling capable, so it must never make them feel slow or stupid. Find every place where the tone works against that.

Look for:
- Words that make a step sound trivial: "simply", "just", "easy", "obviously", "of course", "clearly", "quickly". A student who finds that step hard will feel bad. ("just like the meteor timer", meaning "in the same way as", is fine.)
- Mistakes or errors framed as the student's fault, or no help at all for common mistakes (a nil error, a missing `end`, code in the wrong function).
- Hard parts that aren't flagged as hard. Honesty about difficulty ("this part is tricky") is supportive.
- Praise that's empty ("Great job!", "Awesome!") instead of naming what their game now does.
- Too much cheerfulness, or fake enthusiasm that a teenager would find cringe.
- Talking down: over-explaining something already taught in an earlier part, or a babyish tone.
- Missed chances to tell the student what they've learned to do, in concrete terms, at the end of a part or challenge.
- Optional tasks and "Going Further" ideas written so that skipping them feels like failing.
- Hints that make the student feel they should have known the answer.

Suggest rewrites that are warm, honest, and specific, and that respect the student.
